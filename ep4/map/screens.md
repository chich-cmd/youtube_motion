## 2. Exam passage page (left panel)

- **Panel:** an off-white textured-paper panel that fills the left of the frame, full height (y 0–1080). The right edge is a hard vertical edge at x=1070 (77.5–122.5), x=1026 (127.0–210.75) and x=1039 (214.75–247.5 and 254.5–320.5). Inside it the printed page (white) reaches about x≈855 (in the 1070 layout). The textured margin on the right of the page holds the handwritten notes.
- **Content:** question "34." English passage, "Long before Walt Whitman wrote *Leaves of Grass*, poets had addressed themselves to fame. Horace, Petrarch, Shakespeare, Milton, and Keats all hoped that poetic greatness would grant them a kind of earthly immortality. … Whitman's poet sought ________. In the instability of American democracy, fame would be dependent on celebrity, on the degree to which the people rejoiced in the poet and his work. [3점]". There is a gloss "* rally: 집회" and the options ① a refuge from public attention ② poetic purity out of political chaos ③ immortality in literature itself ④ the approval of his contemporaries ⑤ fame with political celebrities. A black answer underline sits below the options.
- **Handwriting/annotation activity:** the pen strokes appear progressively, synced to the speech.
  - 77.5: page appears. Line 1 has light yellow highlight and orange/red pen circles ("Whitman", "poets") in the first lines.
  - ~88–90: yellow highlight on "addressed themselves to fame", and "poets" circled. ~89–90 the handwritten margin note **"시인들 / 명성 원함"** (dark pen, top-right margin, about x810–990, y30–120) is written. At the same time the big white handwriting caption "시인들 명성 원함!" and "(슥삭슥삭)" appear (section 3).
  - ~96–122: blue pen strokes/brackets on lines 1–3 (blue arc beside the margin note, and blue underlines on "addressed themselves to fame", "Milton, and Keats all hoped").
  - 127 onward (new layout): orange circle around "명성" in the margin note. Black chunk-slash marks ("/" with a hook) go between chunks on lines 1–3 (after "fame", "hoped", "immortality"), and green highlight builds on lines 2–3 ("Horace, Petrarch, Shakespeare, Milton, and Keats all hoped that poetic greatness would grant them a kind of earthly immortality"), ~137–160.

  - 214.75 onward: pink/red highlight on lines 4–8 ("But to this ancient desire to live forever on the page, he added a new sense of fame. Readers would not simply attend to the poet's work; they would be attracted to the greatness of his personality"), with more chunk slashes.
